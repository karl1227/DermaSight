About DermaSight

Supporting skin awareness through preliminary screening

Current local development version. Guest and registered screening records remain on the phone. Cloud screening storage and Firebase integration are planned, not enabled. Opening this page does not record consent or change your account.

DermaSight is a hybrid mobile application developed for the capstone study DermaSight: A Mobile Application for Pigmented Skin Lesion Classification Utilizing Convolutional Neural Networks.

The project was developed by Karl Christopher D. Blockstock, Michelle Ann S. Angeles, Emmanuel M. Espeña and Gerald Elli Ramos of the College of Computer Studies, Technological Institute of the Philippines–Quezon City.

Purpose

DermaSight helps users document a skin concern and obtain a preliminary image-based classification. Its purpose is to support awareness and encourage appropriate professional assessment.

It does not confirm a disease, determine severity or cancer stage, prescribe treatment, or replace a licensed healthcare professional.

How the application works

Users provide screening information, select the lesion’s body location, answer the available symptom questions, and capture or select a photograph.

The information collected includes name, age, sex, lesion location, occupation and history of sunburn. These entries provide a record of the screening context. Collecting them does not, by itself, establish that they are inputs to the image-classification model.

The application prepares the photograph and uses its classification model to suggest a possible lesion category. The model was trained using HAM10000, a research dataset containing dermoscopic images organized into seven categories:

  • Melanoma
  • Basal cell carcinoma
  • Actinic keratoses and intraepithelial carcinoma, including Bowen’s disease
  • Melanocytic nevi
  • Benign keratosis-like lesions
  • Dermatofibroma
  • Vascular lesions

Offline and online operation

Guests use a unique local guest session without creating an account. Screening records and images remain on the device. Registered users can also access locally saved records through a valid restored session.

An internet connection allows users to register or sign in through the account server. Screening records and images still remain on the phone; signing in does not upload them. The email identifies the account, and reports are not automatically emailed. Cloud storage and cross-device screening synchronization are planned, not implemented.

Body locations and model limitations

DermaSight provides multiple body-location options rather than limiting screening to the torso and arms. Selecting a location organizes the screening information; it does not establish equal model performance at every body site.

The model’s training data consists of dermoscopic research images. Performance on ordinary smartphone photographs and across skin tones requires representative evaluation. Research has documented differences in dermatology AI performance across skin-tone groups.

Understanding the result

A possible classification is a suggested match among the model’s supported categories. A confidence score is a model-generated score, not a confirmed diagnosis or a clinically validated probability that the user has cancer.

A reassuring result should not prevent consultation when a skin change is concerning.
